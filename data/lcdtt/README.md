# LCDTT-Lausanne Corpus of Digital Tangut Texts 
A digital corpus of Tangut texts in machine readable format:
- biography_atiśa.txt: Tangut text extracted from Ma Zhouyang (2025) [Two Early Biographical Accounts of Atiśa Preserved in Tangut Sources](https://www.academia.edu/129858497/Two_Early_Biographical_Accounts_of_Ati%C5%9Ba_Preserved_in_Tangut_Sources)
- bodhicaryavatara_1-4.txt: [Tangut text edition](https://www.academia.edu/97954326/Chapters_1_4_of_the_Bodhicary%C4%81vat%C4%81ra) by Kirill Solonin and Arakawa Shintaro, converted to Tangut Unicode from 景永時字體.
- bodhicaryavatara_5-8.txt: [Tangut text edition](https://www.academia.edu/144598049/Bodhicary%C4%81vat%C4%81ra_in_Tangut_Translation_Juan_two_and_three_Chapters_5_8_with_Translation_from_Tangut_and_Notes) by Kirill Solonin and Arakawa Shintaro, converted to Tangut Unicode from 景永時字體.
- saptaguṇvarṇanā_parikathā.txt: Tangut text extracted from Hu Enbo (2024) [Edition of the Saptaguṇavarṇanā parikathā Based on a Newly Identified Sanskrit Manuscript with Special Reference to Its Tangut Translation](https://www.academia.edu/116708208/Edition_of_the_Saptagu%E1%B9%87avar%E1%B9%87an%C4%81_parikath%C4%81_Based_on_a_Newly_Identified_Sanskrit_Manuscript_with_Special_Reference_to_Its_Tangut_Translation)
- tangut_legal_texts.txt: Tangut text extracted from Alan Downes (2018) [PhD thesis "How Does Tangut Work?"](https://www.academia.edu/36787770/How_Does_Tangut_Work_PhD_Thesis)

## In this repository

Snapshot of `bjargal/LCDTT-tangut` at commit `3cb572ac74c67158c7f88ecf37ad81efe87b92ae`
(2026-04-18), read 2026-11-04. The 21 `.txt` files are copied here; the two JSON dictionaries are
not. The README above is LCDTT's, with this section added.

The LCDTT repository states no licence. The project owner granted permission to use the corpus on
2026-11-04. Each text above names the edition or study it was extracted from; those citations are
the authority for the text, and the editions' own terms apply. The `tangut_lfw.json` and
`tangut_unicode.json` dictionaries were not copied.

Used by `scripts/build_tangut_attestation.py` as one source, `lcdtt`. The two Bodhicaryāvatāra files
come from Kirill Solonin and Shintaro Arakawa's edition, whose two ILCAA volumes are CC BY 4.0
(volume 2: <https://hdl.handle.net/10108/0002001372>). The other texts are cited individually above.
The Dragon King file's Chinese parallel comes from BabelStone's page; see `../babelstone/README.md`.

## The road word in the two Jacques texts

`類林.txt` and `慈孝傳.txt` come from Guillaume Jacques's editions, and a lookup by his number puts the
road word on dictionary entry 0021, the elephant 𗵚 (U+17D5A). The road entry is 0020, 𗵘 (U+17D58).
[Babelstone's English index to Li Fanwen's 2008
dictionary](https://www.babelstone.co.uk/Tangut/XHZD_EnglishIndex.html) lists 0021 under *elephant* and
0020 under *road*, and [his index to the 1997
dictionary](https://www.babelstone.co.uk/Tangut/XHZD_Index.html) gives 0020 as tśja 1.19 and 0021 as bju
1.2. So a transcription made from the number carries the elephant wherever the text means 路 or 道.

- The 33 rows of the 類林 table numbered 0021 carry the road word's own rhyme 1.19 and the gloss 路 or 道.
- 韓小忙 2021, 《西夏文詞典・世俗文獻部分》 vol. 5, enters 《類林》16A3 借道 `tsja¹ mjir²` under 0020.
- Jacques's *Textes tangoutes I* sets the road glyph beside number 21 at 2.2, 4.4, 5.4, 9.3, 14.3,
  18.5, 25.2, 28.3, 29.1 and 31.6; the transcription has 𗵚 at that position in nine of them.

`scripts/build_tangut_attestation.py` reads 𗵘 at those places. The files above stay as they were copied,
and the number of places is pinned, so a snapshot that changes it stops the build. At 14.3 the
transcription has a different character there (𗦰); that one place is not corrected.
